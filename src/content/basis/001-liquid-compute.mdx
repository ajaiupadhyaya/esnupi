## Summary

- **What it is.** A four-layer stack for GPU compute: reference indices, a physical order book, an exchange and clearinghouse (PMEX Markets, CFTC application pending), and compute credit. A $15M seed closed September 15, 2026; a $250M prepayment facility with K8 Capital followed on October 6.

## 2. Why compute needs a market

Compute already trades on a curve, and the shape of that curve is a risk premium someone gets paid to carry.

1. **Owners must sell term.** Lenders size GPU loans to contracted revenue, so operators sell one to three years forward to borrow at all. Term buyers are scarce, so term clears at a discount.
2. **Compute cannot be stored.** An unsold hour expires. With no cash-and-carry arbitrage, the forward price is expectation plus risk premium, the same structure as power.
3. **Prices jump.** Hardware loses value on release dates, not smoothly. Residual value plays the role recovery plays in single-name credit.

![B300 price falls from $5.97 on demand to $4.25 for a 36-month commitment](/basis/liquid-compute/term-structure.svg)

The index history shows why hedging matters. LCI-H100-US printed $2.95 at the end of June 2025, fell 38% to $1.84 by late December as Blackwell supply arrived, then rose 40% to $2.57 by August 29, 2026 as inference demand absorbed legacy supply. The same chip paid the short in 2025 and the long in 2026. Silicon Data put H100 at $2.53 to $2.81 and B200 at $5.45 to $6.01 per GPU-hour in mid-October 2026, with both forward curves in backwardation.

## 3. Competitive landscape

The cash-settled futures contract will be a crowded, incumbent-led product; physical delivery, verification and credit are not.

| Player | Building | Index | Physical | Credit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| ICE + Ornn | Futures on H100, H200, B200, RTX 5090 | Ornn (transaction-based) | Ornn Exchange | No |
| Architect (AX) | Perpetual futures on GPU and DRAM prices, live offshore | Ornn | No | No |
| SF Compute | Resellable cluster order book; $40M Series A | Own book | Yes | No |
| Kalshi, Polymarket US | Event contracts on GPU prices | Third-party | No | No |
| **Liquid Compute** | Order book, indices, DCM/DCO, credit | LCI, five chips | **Yes, verified** | **Yes** |

If CME's contract becomes the reference price, Liquid Compute's index becomes a second opinion. The defense is plain: a cash future cannot deliver a cluster, verify one, or be lent against without someone who can repossess and re-let the capacity. The best outcome may be to clear physical against whichever cash contract wins, the way exchange-for-physical works in energy.

## 6. A quant look at the tenor trade

I extended Liquid Compute's own worked example into a simulation, and it supports their thesis: placement risk moves the outcome more than price risk. Selling every hour roughly doubles expected P&L, from $0.62M to $1.23M, while a one-third price hedge trims the downside but leaves the chance of a loss at 44%.

The setup: 64 B300 GPUs bought for 36 months at $4.25 with 30% prepaid ($2.14M), re-let monthly from a $5.30 start. The deterministic grid reproduces their note (+$589K a year at full fill, +$292K at 90%, −$6K at 80%). The simulation adds lognormal price moves at 6% monthly volatility, a −10% release-date jump about every two years, fill that falls when price falls, and $0.15 of basis noise between the index and the holder's own price. The mean month-12 price comes out at $5.11, above the $4.85 forward; selling forward pays that gap as a risk premium, which is why every hedged mean sits below the unhedged one.

![Mean P&L and 5th to 95th percentile range by hedge and placement case](/basis/liquid-compute/tenor-trade.svg)

1. **Placement is the bigger lever.** Full fill adds about $0.61M of expected P&L. No price hedge adds expected value; it only narrows the range. That is the case for the brokerage desk.
2. **Wrong-way correlation is the hidden cost.** Making fill independent of price lifts the mean by $0.19M and cuts the chance of loss from 44% to 36%.
3. **Hedging needs the long end of the curve.** A full 36-month hedge cuts the chance of loss to 14%, but dealers quote mainly in one-to-two-year tenors.

## 8. What I'd build next

1. **Basis study.** Minimum-variance hedge ratios and residual basis in dollars per GPU-hour for three archetypes: an inference company on H100s, an operator long unsold B200s, and a lender marked to residual value.
2. **Residual-value model.** A curve by chip and region, with jumps on release dates, as the missing input for compute credit.
