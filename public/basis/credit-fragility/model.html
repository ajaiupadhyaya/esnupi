The liquidity lab: what a gate buys

A deliberately small, auditable model of a fund paying redemptions by selling illiquid assets. Move the requests, the gate and the sale haircut, and every quarter's accounting updates. The calculation is the one from my Credit Observatory project, unchanged. It does not reproduce any particular fund's terms.

01 · The run

When withdrawals meet illiquid assets

One group of investors asks for its money back. The fund has no cash, so every dollar it pays comes from selling assets below book value, up to the gate each quarter.

Each quarter. The fund pays the smallest of what is still requested, the gate, and what its remaining assets would fetch.
The sale. Assets sold = cash paid ÷ (1 − haircut). Haircut loss = assets sold − cash paid.
The check. Every quarter, 100 = assets left + redemptions paid so far + haircut losses so far.

02 · The gate

Time is not capital

Both charts below rerun your requests and haircut at every gate from 1% to 25%. A tighter gate stretches the timetable. It does not change the bill.

Quarters needed, by gate

Total haircut loss per 100, by gate

That flat line is an identity of this model, not an empirical result. There is no income or price recovery, so waiting cannot help. While assets last, the loss is requests × haircut ÷ (1 − haircut).

03 · The edge

Where the assets run out

Requests go unpaid once they exceed what the whole book can fetch: 100 × (1 − haircut). The gate does not move that line. Cells show unpaid requests per 100 of initial NAV, and the outlined cell is the closest to your settings.

Across: redemption requests, % of NAV. Down: sale haircut.

Sources and limits
  1. Calculation: simulate() from frontend/domain.js, copied unchanged, which the project tests against src/models/liquidity_stress.py. This page matches both.
  2. The default run (30% requests, 10% gate, 15% haircut) reproduces the project's published result: 3 quarters, 5.29 of haircut losses, nothing unpaid, 64.71 of book assets left.
  3. One redemption cohort. No existing cash, debt, income, new requests or price recovery. Gates apply to initial NAV. The simulator has no time limit, so a 1% gate can run for 100 quarters.
  4. Redemptions are paid at book value with no NAV markdown, so in this model the whole haircut falls on the claims that remain.
  5. Requests, gates and haircuts are inputs, not estimates. Nothing here is calibrated to a real fund or a real sale.
