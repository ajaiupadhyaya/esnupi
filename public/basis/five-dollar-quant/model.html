One round trip, leg by leg

A purchase pays half the spread. A sale pays half the spread plus two fees, each with a one-cent minimum. Those minimums are fixed in cents, so their weight depends on the size of the order.

Order size. The engine sizes every order as a weight times 90% of equity, because 10% stays in cash at every tier.
Minimum. An order under $1.00 is rejected and logged. Nothing fills and nothing is charged.
Stress. Scales the spread only, because that is what the code does. The fees stay put.
Default turnover. 2.1 round trips a year is Donchian on SPY at $5: 10 sales in 1,208 test days. Those fees come to 0.84% of capital a year, and removing them from that backtest adds 0.84 points of CAGR.

Published numbers, recomputed on this page

Each row runs one purchase of 100% of deployable capital through the calculation above and sets it beside the figure in the experiment files. Buy-and-hold makes exactly one order, so its whole recorded cost is that purchase. At the defaults, the first row is the buy leg shown above.

Sources and limits
  1. Port of fdq/frictions/config.py and fdq/frictions/emulator.py, with parameters from config/friction_v1.yaml, in capitallimits.
  2. Checked against the repo's own emulator on 20,000 random fills, 20,000 order checks and all 1,462 fills from the three experiments. The largest difference was zero.
  3. The spreads and fee minimums are configured assumptions, and the config calls the fees approximate. No real or paper fill in the repo calibrates them.
  4. This page charges what apply_fill charges. On a full exit the backtest records the sell half-spread but does not deduct it from cash, so at the defaults a backtest pays 0.045¢ less than the round trip shown.
  5. The annual cost multiplies one round trip at today's capital by turnover. It ignores compounding and assumes each round trip is the same size.
  6. Fees apply to sales only. There is no commission and no market impact in this model.
